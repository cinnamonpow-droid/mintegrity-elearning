MICRO-MODULE 1: Overview of Australian FX Market Regulation

There are three main regulators which oversee aspects of FX trading and the FX market in Australia. Click the tabs below to learn about each one:

Australian Securities and Investments Commission
ASIC is Australia’s markets and financial services conduct regulator. Generally, participants who advise, deal, or make a market in financial products need to operate under an Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL).
Reserve Bank of Australia
The central bank of Australia has a market-wide focus on the stability of the financial system, with a particular connection to the FX market.
Australian Transaction Reports and Analysis Centre
Australia’s financial intelligence agency with regulatory responsibility for anti-money laundering, counter-terrorism financing and financial transaction reporting.

MICRO-MODULE 2: Enforcement Outcomes

Following its investigations, ASIC has implemented a series of substantial enforcement outcomes relating to FX.

Tap cards to review key enforcement cases ↺
ASIC Enforceable Undertakings
Failings included: Disclosure of confidential client orders, trading inconsistent with orders, and attempts to unduly influence the market.
Insider Trading in FX
In 2015, two margin FX traders were sentenced to jail for trading on non-public ABS statistical information, netting $7 million.
AUSTRAC Reporting Failure
An FX business was fined $100k and its manager jailed for 10 months for avoiding standard significant cash transaction reports.

MICRO-MODULE 3: Contraventions

The Australian Corporations Act and the ASIC Act both have provisions which are relevant to conduct issues in the FX market.

Tap cards to reveal prohibited behaviors ↺
Insider Trading
Includes trading on inside info, tipping others to trade, disclosing confidential client order info, and acting on confidential info.
Market Manipulation
Manipulating the price (particularly at key fixing times), false trading, and trading with no change in beneficial ownership.
Misleading Conduct
Making false or misleading statements and inducing someone to deal based on those statements.

Knowledge Check

Contraventions of your obligations under your AFS Licence can lead to civil or criminal prosecution against the firm but not an individual.

MICRO-MODULE 4: Conduct and Behaviour

In May 2017, ASIC released Report 525, “Promoting better behaviour: Spot FX”. It details concerns and sets clear guidance on behavior expectations.

Tap cards to compare Concerns vs. Expectations ↺
ASIC Concerns
Lack of training, poor handling of confidential info, vague targets, and failing to detect or address misconduct.
ASIC Expectations
Supervisors model expected standards, foster an environment to challenge inappropriate practices, and impose visible sanctions.
FX Global Code
Released in mid-2017, a key document setting out high-level regulatory expectations. All FX market individuals must understand it.

Knowledge Check

ASIC expects that supervisors should be held to account where their behaviour has not been adequate.

MICRO-MODULE 5: Rules and Regulations

All designated services in financial products are covered by AUSTRAC’S requirements, including making a market and dealing with clients in FX and derivatives.

The Wholesale Test

In Australian regulation there is a distinction between Retail and Wholesale clients. Retail clients have additional protections. A financial product or service is provided to a person as a retail client unless one or more of the following apply (click to expand):

The price for the provision of the product is $500k or greater.

The product is for use in connection to a business, which is not a small business, being 20 or more employees or 100 or more for a manufacturer of goods.

The client has a certificate from a qualified accountant setting out that the client had net assets of at least $2.5m or an income of at least $250k over the last two years.

The person is a Professional Investor (e.g., licensed financial services firms, trustees of super funds with $10m+ assets) or a Sophisticated Investor with relevant investing experience acknowledged prior to product provision.

Knowledge Check

If you deal with clients in FX, as long as you are enrolled with AUSTRAC, there is no need to implement an AML/CTF Program.
