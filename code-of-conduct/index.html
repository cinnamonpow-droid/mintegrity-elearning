MODULE 1: Overview of Conduct Obligations

ASIC believes that a code of conduct is a pinnacle of industry self-regulatory initiatives. Click the tabs below to explore the role and criteria of codes:

Purpose & Roles of Codes
Codes are essentially a set of enforceable rules that delivers measurable benefit to consumers. They improve consumer trust, support dispute resolution, and provide certainty. Their primary role is to raise standards and complement legislative requirements. An effective code addresses specific industry issues not covered by legislation and clarifies what needs to be done to comply.
Code Criteria
While not mandatory, ASIC's criteria for approving a code include: consistency with the Corporations ActCorporations Act 2001 (Cth), appropriate development process, adequate enforceability (dispute resolution/sanctions), effective monitoring/reporting, and independent regular reviews.

AFSL & Market Participant Obligations

Under s912A of the Corporations Act, an AFS licenseeA person who holds an Australian financial services licence under s913B of the Corporations Act must do all things necessary to ensure financial services are provided efficiently, honestly and fairly. They must have adequate arrangements for managing conflicts of interest, comply with licence conditions, maintain competence, have adequate risk and compliance arrangements, ensure representatives are trained, and have a dispute resolution system for Retail clientsA client as defined in s761G of the Corporations Act and Div 2 of Pt 7.1 of the Corporations Regulations.

Under s912D and s912DAA, licensees must notify ASIC within 30 Business Days of a reportable situation, including significant breaches, investigations lasting over 30 days, or conduct constituting gross negligence or serious fraud. Furthermore, Directors must act with due care, diligence, and in the best interests of the corporation.

ASIC Market Integrity Rules require appropriate management structures, operations reasonably designed to achieve compliance, and supervisory staff with sufficient seniority. Market ParticipantsA participant in the Market admitted under the Market Operating Rules must only hire persons of good fame and character. Under Rule 2.1.5 and 2.1.6, they must not engage in Unprofessional Conduct and are responsible for all actions and omissions of all employees.

When providing personal advice to retail clients, you must act in their best interests (s961B), provide appropriate advice (s961G), warn if advice is based on incomplete info (s961H), and prioritise the client's interests (s961J). A FSGFinancial Services Guide must be prepared and given to the client before the service is provided.

MODULE 2: Conduct Risk

Conduct Risk is defined as the risk of inappropriate, unethical or unlawful behaviour on the part of an organisation’s management or employees. It should be actively identified and monitored by compliance and senior management.

ASIC believes there are four key drivers of culture: Leadership & professionalism, governance & individual accountability, performance & consequence management, and conduct & culture training.

ASIC observed stronger accountability when boards showed active management by holding senior management to account when operating outside risk appetites. Better practices include robust documented policies, tracking culture/conduct risk, distinguishing global vs regional risks, using the three lines of defence, and having direct engagement from the compliance function in consequence decisions.

Good conduct was incentivised when performance reviews contained key indicators for compliance, career progression was aligned with positive conduct, and downward adjustments to remuneration were applied for breaches. Furthermore, conduct and culture training should be ongoing for both new and existing employees, utilizing real-life examples of misconduct as case studies.

ASIC's review found poorer practices included inadequate distinction between conduct risk and operational risk controls; instances where conduct did not inform individual remuneration; limited influence of compliance in decision-making; and failure to firmly embed conduct training into regular schedules.

MODULE 3: Responsible Behaviour

All AFSLAn Australian Financial Services Licence granted under s913B of the Corporations Act holders are expected to act professionally and ethically in carrying out their duties. Review the categories of responsible behavior below.

Privacy
Protecting client and firm information is integral. Firms should not misuse information obtained as a consequence of client relationships for personal gain. When collecting and safeguarding information, firms should not share non-public information — accidentally or intentionally — with a third party or colleague who does not have a business need to know.
Public Representation
Firms must respect individual freedom of expression. However, an individual must exercise personal responsibility and care during or outside of work (including comments made online or to media) to ensure that any casual comment does not reveal private or confidential information, or compromise the integrity and reputation of the firm.
Social Media & Communications
Firms should not allow employees to use social media platforms to conduct business or discuss client matters. Employees must take care to separate their personal roles from their business position when communicating online, and to not presume to speak for the business when not authorized.

MODULE 4: Code of Conduct Content

Whilst there is no one size fits all in regards to designing a Code of Conduct, the following information outlines content that has been evidenced as best practice.

Employees must obey the law and engage in behaviour to act honestly, efficiently, and fairly. There is zero tolerance for illegal acts, harassment, bullying, or sexual harassment. A financial firm should provide a safe, healthy work environment that is inclusive and respectful of all employees’ differences. Unlawful discrimination or victimisation will not be tolerated.

Employees must not use their position for private advantage. Outside employment/directorships should be disclosed and approved to avoid interference with independent judgment. The term ‘soft dollar’ means any benefit received other than a basic cash commission (e.g., subsidised business equipment, overseas conferences). The law generally requires these be disclosed in the FSG and Statement of Advice. Firms must outline permissible gift limits and strictly forbid kickbacks or secret commissions.

Employees should not participate in creating any false record or communication, whether internal or external. This includes false expense, attendance, or production statements, as well as false advertising or deceptive marketing practices.

All firms should have a Privacy Policy. Personal information must only be collected directly from the person whenever possible, retained only for as long as necessary, physically secured, and strictly limited regarding internal access. Personal info must only be used for the purposes for which it was originally obtained.

MODULE 5: FAS’s Code of Ethics

The FASFinancial Adviser Standards Code of Ethics is a principles-based standard designed to professionalise the sector. Advisers must always act in a way that promotes the core values: Trustworthiness, Competence, Honesty, Fairness, and Diligence.

Standard 1: You must act in accordance with all applicable laws, including this Code, and not try to avoid or circumvent their intent.
Standard 2: You must act with integrity and in the best interests of each of your clients.
Standard 3: You must not advise, refer or act in any other manner where you have a conflict of interest or duty. Any conflict must be disclosed and you must not act.

Standard 4: You may act for a client only with the client’s free, prior and informed consent, explaining services, terms, and privacy clearly.
Standard 5: All advice and product recommendations must be in the best interests of the client and appropriate to their individual circumstances.
Standard 6: You must take into account the broad effects arising from the client acting on your advice and actively consider their broader, long-term interests.

Standard 7: The client must give informed consent to all benefits/fees. You must satisfy yourself that fees and charges are fair, reasonable, and represent value for money.
Standard 8: You must ensure that your records of clients are kept in a form that is complete and accurate.
Standard 9: All advice you give, and products you recommend, must be offered in good faith, with competence, and be neither misleading nor deceptive.

Standard 10: You must develop, maintain and apply a high level of relevant knowledge and skills.
Standard 11: You must cooperate with ASIC and monitoring bodies in any investigation of a breach or potential breach.
Standard 12: Individually and in cooperation with peers, you must uphold and promote the ethical standards of the profession and hold each other accountable.

MODULE 6: AFMA Code of Conduct

The AFMAAustralian Financial Markets Association Code outlines ethical principles for acceptable behaviour in financial markets. The overarching aim is to set high standards when conducting business in OTC and exchange-traded markets.

EP 1-3: Community, Culture & Laws
Conduct affairs to serve the broader community. Boards and senior management must establish a culture setting clear expectations about conduct. Members must observe laws. This includes having "Chinese walls" to quarantine employees with inside information, maintaining an AML/CTF program to "Know Your Client", and avoiding anti-competitive behaviour like cartels or exclusive dealing.
EP 4-6: Fairness & Professionalism
Act fairly with clients. Under FOFA, members servicing retail clients have a higher duty of care and must pay close attention to vulnerable client groups (e.g., aged, hearing impaired, or those with poor English). Members must equip staff to perform with integrity, managing conflicts in remuneration, controlling personal account trading, and ensuring judgment is not impaired by illicit drugs or alcohol.
EP 7-9: Respect, Authority & Records
Behaviour towards colleagues must be respectful; harassment is not tolerated. Staff must act within delegated authority and clearly defined risk parameters. Members must ensure the integrity and confidentiality of records, retaining data in readily accessible formats for minimum statutory periods and destroying it only via defined policies.
EP 10-12: Conflicts, Standards & Reputation
Identify and manage conflicts, encouraging a culture of whistleblowing for market irregularities. Observe market standards: front office traders should not take part in settlement (Segregation of Duties), and system access to trading platforms must be strictly governed. Members should anticipate disruptions with a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) and maintain careful media policies.

MODULE 7: SIAA Code of Ethical Conduct

The SIAAStockbrokers and Investment Advisers Association Code provides guidance to maintain and improve ethical behaviour in the stockbroking and investment advice profession.

Obey the law & Integrity: Members must obey all laws, act unbiased, and be personally responsible for their conduct. Discrimination based on gender, race, religion, or sexual identity is forbidden.
Respect Client Rights & Confidentiality: Provide adequate information for informed consent regarding work and fees. Confidential client information must only be disclosed as authorised by the client, required by law, or to the Association during disciplinary reviews.

Competence: Maintain knowledge through continuing professional development (CPD). Principal Members must take reasonable action to ensure staff are competent and provide required training and supervision.
Fair Market: Compete fairly without taking unfair advantage or engaging in unconscionable conduct. Principal members must have adequate arrangements to minimize conflicts of interest.

Members must report illegal conduct to appropriate authorities where disclosure is protected by law. Crucially, members must not discriminate or take adverse action against a person who discloses illegal conduct. Furthermore, members must obtain required Professional Indemnity Insurance cover.

MODULE 8: Consequences of Misconduct

If conduct is a potential breach of the Corporations Act, ASIC may pursue civil, criminal or administrative action. ASIC can remove the firm’s AFSL, and individuals could be subject to suspensions, banning, fines, and gaol time. Internal breaches may be addressed by Senior Management, HR, and Compliance, and your firm may have to self-report the matter. If the conduct breaches ASIC Market Integrity Rules, the MDPASIC’s Markets Disciplinary Panel may issue infringement notices, penalties, or public censures against the Market Participant.

It is important to speak up about potential breaches even if it does not directly affect you. Suspected breaches should immediately be reported to a manager, Compliance, or HR. Whistleblowers may be offered protection by laws such as the Fair Work Act 2009, the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013, and Whistleblowing protections RG170.

The Scenario: Mike is an adviser with Fantastic Advice Pty Ltd, a subsidiary of Big Bank Ltd. He advises Max and Olivia to set up an SMSF to buy a $1.5m bed and breakfast, rolling over their $300k combined super, and purchasing high-premium life insurance from a related body corporate to cover the massive debt. Max and Olivia establish the SMSF, paying $5,000 upfront and $3,000 ongoing. Big Bank receives $4,000 in upfront commissions, helping Mike hit his bonus target. Later, Big Bank tells Max and Olivia their situation cannot actually support the loan they need.

The Breaches: Mike breached FAS Standards 2, 5, and 6 by failing to provide advice in his clients' best interests and ignoring long-term circumstances. He breached Standard 3 by acting to derive an inappropriate personal advantage (bonuses and commissions). Finally, he breached Standard 7 as the excessive fees did not represent value for money for Max and Olivia.
